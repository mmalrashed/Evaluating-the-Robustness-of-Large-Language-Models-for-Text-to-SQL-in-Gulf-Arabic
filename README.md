# Evaluating-the-Robustness-of-Large-Language-Models-for-Text-to-SQL-in-Gulf-Arabic

Clean benchmarks demonstrate phenomenal performance for large language models (LLMs) for Text-to-SQL tasks. However, the robust performance of LLMs has not been adequately evaluated under adversarial and dialectal variance. This work explores the performance of three different LLMs (ChatGPT, DeepSeek, and Gemini) when performing various Khaleeji Arabic Text-to-SQL tasks, and evaluates their adversarial robustness under a comprehensive suite of character, word, and sentence level adversarial attacks. Performance is measured using execution accuracy, exact match, logical form accuracy, and adversarially oriented metrics including accuracy drop and fine-grained error-type categorization. Results indicate that adversarial perturbations significantly decrease ChatGPT's performance by causing execution accuracy under 5\% in sentence-level attacks with no schema, along with a high occurrence of table-level errors. Character-level attacks cause moderate performance reductions, while word- and sentence-level attacks lead to greater declines in execution accuracy, with sentence-level attacks having the strongest impact on schema linking and SQL generation. DeepSeek and Gemini were comparatively more robust than ChatGPT by retaining execution accuracy values generally in excess of approximately 55--60\%. Schema grounding greatly increases model robustness but does not reduce attribute-level error occurrences as the most common mode of failure among models. Generally, LLM-based Text-to-SQL systems for Khaleeji Arabic have considerable robustness deficiencies, indicating the critical importance of deploying adversarially robust models for applications that are sensitive to dialects.
